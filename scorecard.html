How these templates score against best-in-class LinkedIn outreach

Scored against the largest 2025–26 outreach datasets (Expandi 13.2M requests, Belkins 15–20M touchpoints, Reachium 180K requests, ReactIn 80K) and 2026 B2B webinar benchmarks (ON24/GoTo/BrightTALK via Digital Applied, Goldcast, Contrast). The objective is not a booked demo. It is a registered, reminded, attending prospect at webinar.bighammerai.com (Thu 15 Oct 2026, 12:00 PM ET, 45 min + Q&A).

The first two numbers are sequence-level judgments: how the campaigns work as systems (ordering, branching, what reaches whom). 3. Where it beats the benchmark

  • A0 note follows the data exactly: context first, no link, no pitch, under 200 characters, and a bare request when there is no context. Most "world-class" template libraries still recommend generic notes; the data says those lose to no note at all.
  • B1 is the single best message in the set. A short, answerable question to 1st-degree connections is the highest-reply motion LinkedIn has (16.9% average, 25–35% when the sender is known), and the "or is it under control" clause is textbook permission-to-say-no.
  • A2 is proof, not pitch. Naming system.compute.node_timeline and a concrete threshold does what a case-study link cannot on mobile: it makes the sender credible in one screen.
  • Every message ends in a question or an easy exit. Branching on reply, killing on any reply, and a 5-touch cap are the controls that keep a seat's reply rate above 10% instead of drifting toward the 3% template floor.
  • A5 already carries the one line that reduces no-shows ("register even if the time doesn't work and you'll get the recording").

4. Where it loses to the benchmark, given the webinar goal

Fix 1 — Move the webinar forward in Campaign A (biggest lever).Today the masterclass is A5: day 25, conditional on silence. A free 45-minute session is a lower-friction ask than A4's 30-minute demo that needs a workspace id, so it should come before the demo, not after. Recommended order: A1 (thank-you) → A2 (CPU proof) → A3 (queries) → A4 = masterclass invite → A5 = demo offer, only if they engaged. Expected effect: the webinar CTA reaches 100% of accepted connections instead of the ~70% who never reply.
Fix 2 — Give Campaign B a webinar exit.B2-hot offers the queries and the demo; the masterclass is absent. For a "yes" from a 1st-degree connection the masterclass is the natural middle step. The B1 · LIVE webinar-led card on the preview page is the direct version; at minimum add a third option to B2-hot: "or sit in on the live teardown on {{webinar_date}}: {{webinar_url}}".
Fix 3 — Add the reminder layer to Campaign C (worth ~27% more attendees).Campaign C starts the day after the event. Attendance is decided before it. Add two LinkedIn touches to registrants: C0-a the day before, C0-b two hours before. Suggested copy: 
{{first_name}} - tomorrow, 12pm ET. One thing to do before it if you have five minutes: pull cpu_user_percent + cpu_system_percent from system.compute.node_timeline for last week. You'll know within the first ten minutes whether the 20% / 40% threshold bites at {{company}}.

Recording goes to everyone registered either way.
Starting in two hours. Link's in your calendar invite, or here: {{webinar_url}}

Bring one number if you can: your biggest all-purpose cluster's average CPU last month. It'll make the Q&A worth your time.
Fix 4 — Tighten the two long messages.A3 (both versions) and A2 run 500–640 characters; the response-rate cliff in LinkedIn's own data starts at 400. Neither needs to lose its substance: A3 can drop the second "Genuinely no strings" paragraph to a single line, and A2 can drop the final two sentences. See the counts in the table above.
Fix 5 — C2 v2 (to approve) scores below the version it replaces.The previous C2 named the person, stated the fact, and handed over the recording in 330 characters. The new one drops the first-name merge, opens with a joke that needs the reader to already like you, and puts a "75%" claim in the second line, which is the one thing a no-show has not earned yet. If the team wants the lighter tone, keep the name and cut the claim: "{{first_name}} - your calendar won yesterday, which is fair. Recording's here: {{replay_url}}. The short version: seven read-only queries, four findings, about half the savings need no migration at all." Also: {{replay_url}} has no live page yet; until it does, C2 cannot ship.

Smaller items

  • Consistency with the live page. A5 says "30 minutes of teardown, 15 of Q&A"; the registration page says 45 minutes plus live Q&A, 12:00 PM ET. Match the page.
  • Time zones. The page lists five time zones. Include the one that matches the prospect's location in {{webinar_date}} (e.g. "Thu 15 Oct, 5pm UK").
  • Link previews. LinkedIn unfurls the first URL into a card. The assessment card reads well; the Calendly card is generic. Consider removing the preview on demo asks and keeping it on the webinar link, where the page title does the selling.
  • A3 v2 opener. "Guess what happens from there?" carries the continuity nicely but reads as a hook. "That CPU check is one piece of a bigger assessment" works as the first line on its own.
  • Em dashes. The revised copy removes them (a sensible AI-tell precaution). Every former em-dash position renders as a spaced hyphen in the previews; pick a house style (comma, hyphen or "~") once and apply it everywhere.
  • Volume. Acceptance peaks at 10–19 requests per seat per day (32%) and falls at 20–29 (26.7%). The doc's 20–25/day is at the top of that band; 15/day per seat is the safer default.
