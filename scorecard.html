How these templates score against best-in-class LinkedIn outreach

Scored against the largest 2025–26 outreach datasets (Expandi 13.2M requests, Belkins 15–20M touchpoints, Reachium 180K requests, ReactIn 80K) and 2026 B2B webinar benchmarks (ON24/GoTo/BrightTALK via Digital Applied, Goldcast, Contrast). The objective is not a booked demo. It is a registered, reminded, attending prospect at webinar.bighammerai.com (Thu 15 Oct 2026, 12:00 PM ET, 45 min + Q&A).

All three headline numbers are computed from the per-message table in section 2 (weights: craft 45%, webinar alignment 35%, link/CTA hygiene 10%, operability without AI research 10%; the last two are near-constant, so the overall is effectively 0.45 × craft + 0.35 × webinar + 1.8). The webinar axis runs low partly by design: most messages have no reason to mention the event. This scorecard was checked by a second review pass that re-fetched every cited source; corrections from that pass are incorporated below.

3. Where it beats the benchmark

  • A0 note follows the data: context first, no link, no pitch, under 200 characters, and a bare request when there is no context. Many template libraries still recommend generic notes; every dataset above says those lose to no note at all.
  • B1 is the single best message in the set. A short, answerable question to 1st-degree connections is one of the highest-reply motions on LinkedIn (12.2% vs 7.2% across all touchpoints in Belkins' data), and the "or is it under control" clause is textbook permission-to-say-no.
  • A2 is proof, not pitch. Naming system.compute.node_timeline and a concrete threshold does what a case-study link cannot on mobile: it makes the sender credible in one screen.
  • Every message ends in a question or an easy exit. Branching on reply, killing on any reply, and a 5-touch cap are the controls that keep a seat's reply rate at the 10% platform average or above instead of drifting toward the 3% that connection-note pitches get.
  • A5 already lowers the registration barrier ("register even if the time doesn't work and you'll get the recording"). Note the trade-off: that line helps registrations and may cost live attendance; the registration page does not promise a recording.

4. Where it loses to the benchmark, given the webinar goal

Fix 0 — The page and the calendar (blocking).Three things gate attendance before any copy does. (a) The raw HTML of webinar.bighammerai.com still says "June 18, 2026 · 11:00 AM ET"; only the in-browser render shows 15 October, 12:00 PM ET. LinkedIn's link unfurl, search engines and anything without JavaScript therefore show a past event. Fix the served HTML before any message carrying {{webinar_url}} ships. (b) The Calendly demo link used in A4, B2-hot and C3 renders "This calendar is currently unavailable." (c) Day offsets, not dates, drive the sequence: with a 21 September start, A4 lands on Saturday 3 October and A5 on Friday 16 October, the day after the event. Anchor the invite to the calendar instead: every accepted connection gets the masterclass message by T−7, whatever step they are on.
Fix 1 — Move the webinar forward in Campaign A (applied 19 Sep: the masterclass CTA now sits in A2, the assessment in A3, the BigHammer offer in A4).Today the masterclass is A5: day 25, conditional on silence. A free 45-minute session is a lower-friction ask than A4's 30-minute demo that needs a workspace id, so it should come before the demo, not after. Recommended order: A1 (thank-you) → A2 (CPU proof) → A3 (queries) → A4 = masterclass invite → A5 = demo offer, only if they engaged. Expected effect: the webinar CTA reaches every accepted connection instead of only the ~90% who never replied (at a 10% reply rate) and who happen to still be in sequence on day 25.
Fix 2 — Give Campaign B a webinar exit (applied 19 Sep: B2-hot offers the masterclass as option 2; B2-cold and B3-park carry the link).B2-hot offers the queries and the demo; the masterclass is absent. For a "yes" from a 1st-degree connection the masterclass is the natural middle step. The B1 · LIVE webinar-led card on the preview page is the direct version; at minimum add a third option to B2-hot: "or sit in on the live teardown on {{webinar_date}}: {{webinar_url}}".
Fix 3 — Put the reminder layer in place (the +27% lever, measured on email).Campaign C starts the day after the event; attendance is decided before it. Registration is an external form that captures a work email and promises "calendar invite and reminder", so first confirm the email reminders actually fire at −1 day, −1 hour and −5 minutes, since that is where the +27% is measured. Then mirror the session as a LinkedIn Event, which gives native invites and LinkedIn's own reminders, and add two LinkedIn touches to registrants you can match to a profile: C0-a the day before, C0-b two hours before. Suggested copy (deliberately no recording promise, since the goal is live seats): 
{{first_name}} - tomorrow, 12pm ET. One thing to do before it if you have five minutes: pull cpu_user_percent + cpu_system_percent from system.compute.node_timeline for last week. You'll know within the first ten minutes whether the 20% / 40% threshold bites at {{company}}.
Starting in two hours. Link's in your calendar invite, or here: {{webinar_url}}

Bring one number if you can: your biggest all-purpose cluster's average CPU last month. It'll make the Q&A worth your time.
Fix 4 — Length (reply rate, not attendance).The 19 Sep revision deliberately traded length for context: A2, A3, A4 and B2-hot now run 950–1,100 characters; LinkedIn's own InMail data puts the response-rate cliff at 400. Neither needs to lose its substance: A3 can cut the closing paragraph to one line, and A2 can drop the final two sentences. See the counts in the table above.
Fix 5 — C2 v2 (to approve) scores below the version it replaces (post-event, so no effect on attendance).The new version drops the first-name merge, and its second line does not parse: "But you still couldn't make it to reduce your databricks costs up to 75%, but Bighammer has got you!" has two "but"s, lowercases the product name and misspells the brand. The previous version said the same thing plainly, with the name, in roughly the same length (321 vs 339 characters). If the team wants the lighter tone, keep the name and fix the sentence: "{{first_name}} - your calendar won yesterday, which is fair. Recording's here: {{replay_url}}. The short version: seven read-only queries, four findings, about half the savings need no migration at all." Both versions depend on {{replay_url}}, which has no live page yet.

Smaller items

  • Consistency with the live page. A5 says "30 minutes of teardown, 15 of Q&A"; the registration page says 45 minutes plus live Q&A, 12:00 PM ET. Match the page.
  • Time zones. The page lists five time zones. Include the one that matches the prospect's location in {{webinar_date}} (e.g. "Thu 15 Oct, 5pm UK").
  • Link previews. LinkedIn unfurls the first URL into a card from the page's served HTML. The assessment card reads well; the Calendly card is generic; the webinar card will carry the stale June date until Fix 0(a) is done.
  • A3 v2 opener. "Guess what happens from there?" carries the continuity nicely but reads as a hook. "That CPU check is one piece of a bigger assessment" works as the first line on its own.
  • Em dashes. The revised copy removes them (a sensible AI-tell precaution). Every former em-dash position renders as a spaced hyphen in the previews; pick a house style (comma, hyphen or "~") once and apply it everywhere.
  • Volume. Acceptance peaks at 10–19 requests per seat per day (32%) and falls at 20–29 (26.7%). The doc's 20–25/day is at the top of that band; 15/day per seat is the safer default.
